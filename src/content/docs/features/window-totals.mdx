A storm total is a question about a window, not about hours. `totals=` asks
the server for the distribution of every member's own total over a window:

```text
totals=<precipitation|snowfall>:<window>[,…]
```

<Example task="storm-total" op="getLocationForecast" />

Each `window_totals[]` entry is a `WindowTotal`: `variable`,
`window_start_hours`, `window_end_hours`, `from`, `to`, `members` and
`quantiles`. Show it the way the NWS words snowfall ranges: "most likely
`p50`, at least `p10`, could reach `p90`".

- **How a window is counted.** A member's precipitation or snowfall value is
  the total over the hours ending at its `valid_time`; a bucket that straddles
  either end of the window counts its share inside. A member counts only when
  its values cover the whole window, and a total needs at least two such
  members.
- **`quantiles` is `null`** when the window is not covered: ask a larger
  `max_hours`.
- **Windows** use the same syntax as [`prob=`](/features/probabilities/):
  `<N>h` or `<A>-<B>h`, up to 360 h. Send the list sorted by variable, then
  window.

This is the only storm total the SDKs offer, because it is the only correct
one: the sum of hourly p90s is not the p90 of the total. The
[worked example](/concepts/adding-quantiles/) shows how far apart they are.

The fixed windows in `forecast.snow_outlook.quantiles` (24 h, 48 h, 7 days)
are computed the same way and come on every forecast.
