An ensemble forecast runs the weather models many times with slightly
different starting conditions and model physics. Each run is a **member**.
Ourea pools the members of up to five ensembles (`model_run.total_members`
says how many), calibrates them, and summarizes every variable at every hour as
a distribution, the `Quantiles` object:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `p10` | 10th percentile: one member in ten is at or below it. A realistic low end. |
| `p25` | 25th percentile. |
| `p50` | The median: half the members above, half below. The headline for skewed quantities such as precipitation and snowfall. |
| `p75` | 75th percentile. |
| `p90` | 90th percentile: one member in ten is above it. A realistic high end. |
| `mean` | The average of the members. For precipitation and snowfall it is pulled up by a few wet members, so it is not the headline at a point. |
| `min`, `max` | The most extreme members: often outliers. Present where the statistic has them. |

Percentiles are over the ensemble members (Hyndman-Fan type 7 where computed
from members).

## How to show them

- **Range:** p10 to p90. Eight members in ten fall inside it.
- **Headline:** p50 for amounts. For temperature the median and the mean are
  close; for precipitation, prefer p50 and pair it with a probability, because
  the median can be 0 on a showery day when rain is still likely.
- **Words:** use the National Weather Service's wording for ranges:
  "Most likely 8 in, at least 4 in, could reach 14 in" (p50, p10, p90). Never
  a bare "4-14 in".

This is what a served forecast's quantiles look like over three days: the
p10 to p90 band holds eight members in ten, the p25 to p75 band half of them,
and the line is the median.

## Things that look like bugs but aren't

- **Wind direction is circular.** It is not a quantile ladder: `wind_direction`
  carries the circular `mean`, `stddev` and the arcs holding 50 % and 90 % of
  the members, `sector_50` and `sector_90`, each running clockwise from
  `from` to `to`, so `to` can be numerically less than `from` (a northerly
  hour: `from` 340, `to` 10). The SDKs pass these through and never "fix"
  them.
- **A missing field is absent, not 0.** An SDK never fills it in.
- **The SDKs never interpolate.** A range is offered only for the pairs the
  server serves (p10 to p90, p25 to p75).
