In the mountains, a few hundred metres change the answer: temperature drops
with height, the snow level decides rain or snow, and snowfall depends on
both. Every Ourea forecast is computed **at a stated elevation**.

## Which elevation

- **A named location** is forecast at its primary elevation,
  `location.elevation_m` (always metres).
- **A multi-elevation location** (a ski area's base, mid and summit) has named
  points; ask for their forecasts with `elevations=all` or by name, and each
  comes back as a full forecast in `elevation_forecasts[]`. See
  [Location info](/features/location-info/#elevation-bands).
- **A coordinate forecast** (Pro) uses `elevation_m` when you send it (it
  always wins), else the USGS 3DEP 1/3 arc-second (about 10 m) DEM elevation of
  the quantized point. `point.elevation_source` says which. Where the DEM has
  no value, the request is `400 ELEVATION_REQUIRED`: the server never guesses.

## What it changes

Each model's 2 m temperature and dewpoint are carried from the model's own
terrain height to the forecast elevation. The elevation then drives the
snow/rain phase, snowfall, and the elevation-keyed calibration. Pass
`elevation_m` when you care about a specific height that is not the ground at
the quantized point: a summit, a lift top, a roof.

Ensemble members are reported at the same elevation as the forecast they
belong to.

## Units

`elevation_m` is always metres. Elevations elsewhere (`point.elevation`,
`elevation_forecasts[].elevation`, the snow level, the freezing level) are in
the response's length unit: metres, or feet with `units=imperial`.
